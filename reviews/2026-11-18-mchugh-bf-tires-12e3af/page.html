Check your tires before you shop for a deal

A sale only helps if you need tires. A few quick checks tell you where you stand.

Tread depth: try the penny test

NHTSA says tires are not safe and should be replaced when the tread is worn down to 2/32 of an inch. To check, put a penny into the tread with Lincoln’s head upside down and facing you. If you can see the top of Lincoln’s head, it is time to replace your tires. Check the tread at least once a month when you check pressure.

Air pressure and the TPMS light

NHTSA recommends checking the pressure of all your tires, including the spare, at least once a month when the tires are cold. The correct pressure is on the Tire and Loading Information Label on the driver’s door edge or doorjamb, or in your owner’s manual. Newer vehicles have a tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS), but it only warns you when a tire is significantly underinflated, so keep checking monthly.

Rotation

Rotate your tires on the schedule in your owner’s manual, which also gives the pattern for your vehicle. Our Mopar Express Lane lists a tire rotation with brake inspection and a tire wear and pressure check.

All-season or winter tires?

NHTSA describes the main choices this way: all-season tires handle a variety of road conditions and have some mud and snow capability; winter tires are more effective than all-season tires in deep snow; summer tires are not designed for temperatures below freezing or for snow and ice. NHTSA also says the right choice varies with where you live and the weather you typically drive in.

Ask yourself:

  • Do I have to drive when roads are snow-covered or icy, or can I wait?
  • Do my current tires still have good tread?
  • Do I drive mostly in town, on I-70, or on back roads?

Four-wheel and all-wheel drive help you get moving, but your tires are what stop and steer your Jeep, Ram or Dodge. Before you head out in winter weather, check current road conditions on OHGO, which shows real-time traffic and winter road conditions.

How to compare Black Friday tire deals

Two offers that look alike can cost different amounts once the tires are on your vehicle. Ask each shop:

  • What is the total installed price? Ask what is included: mounting, balancing, valve stems and old-tire disposal.
  • How does the rebate work? Read the requirements and the deadline, and ask how and when it is paid.
  • Is the size and load rating right for my vehicle? Match the label on your driver’s door edge or your owner’s manual.
  • Are the tires in stock? Ask when they can be installed.
  • Is TPMS sensor service or an alignment recommended? Ask what each costs.
  • What does the warranty cover?

How do I know when my tires need replacing?

NHTSA says tires are not safe and should be replaced when the tread is worn down to 2/32 of an inch. Place a penny in the tread with Lincoln’s head upside down and facing you. If you can see the top of Lincoln’s head, replace your tires. Check tread at least once a month when you check pressure.

Do I need winter tires in Zanesville?

It depends on how and when you drive. NHTSA says all-season tires handle a variety of road conditions with some mud and snow capability, and winter tires are more effective than all-season tires in deep snow. If you must drive when roads are snow-covered or icy, ask us about winter tires for your vehicle.

How often should I rotate my tires?

Rotate your tires on the schedule in your owner’s manual, which also gives the pattern for your vehicle. NHTSA says to rotate sooner if uneven wear appears. Our Mopar Express Lane lists a tire rotation with brake inspection.

What does the tire pressure (TPMS) light mean?

NHTSA says the TPMS symbol means at least one tire is significantly underinflated. Inspect your tires and check the pressure as soon as possible. The light will go out after the tires are properly inflated. Even without a warning, check pressure monthly.
